You: Start this on whichever account has the most left.

Every account, with its limits in view.

Yardlet shows each account's session and weekly limits, with more than one account per provider. On Automatic, a new task or subagent goes on the account with the most room left, and tasks started together spread across your accounts.

From the blog: starting each task on the account with room left.

You: Have Codex review what Claude Code just wrote.

Your agents hand work to each other.

One task can read another, message it while it works, start a task under itself on any agent and account, or run a subagent on another agent and get its answer. Work in another project asks you first.

Forwarded approvals carry your exact words

When one agent passes your approval to another, Yardlet sends your own words with the time you sent them, checks that they are in your messages in that task, and refuses to send if they are not.

Yardlet itself is built this way: one session runs the rest.

You: Show me what changed, then merge it.

Review it next to the conversation, then merge.

Diffs of the last turn, file restore and fork, a terminal and a files pane sit next to the conversation. The pull request card follows checks through your own gh, offers Fix CI when one fails, and merges when it is ready.

You: Let me follow tasks and answer approvals while I'm out.

Follow tasks and answer approvals from your Android phone.

The app pairs with your Mac by QR code. On the same network it connects directly; away from it, through Yardlet's relay, end-to-end encrypted, and the relay can neither read nor store the messages.

The Android app is in testing, for Android 10 and later. In Yardlet on your Mac, open Settings › Remote, turn on Allow remote connections, click Pair… beside Pair a device and scan the code with the app. To update, install a newer APK over it or follow the android-v releases in Obtainium; the app tells you when one is out.

You: What do I risk by trying it?

Your agents, sessions and data stay where they are.

Sessions stay with the agent

Every session stays in the agent's own store on your Mac. Start a task in Yardlet, resume it in the Codex app or Terminal, and switch back anytime.

Stop anytime

Yardlet hands an agent its own MCP tools when it starts it, and you can turn them off. It writes nothing into the agents' settings, so quit or remove it and everything is as it was.

Nothing goes to Yardlet's developer

No analytics and no server that receives your work. The only online services are an update check and, if you pair a phone or another Mac, the end-to-end encrypted relay.

What goes where
Your AI providers
What an agent reads, including through a connector, goes to that agent's provider under your account. Training follows that account's settings.
Services you connect
Calls go straight from your Mac to the service. Sign-ins are kept in the macOS Keychain.
yardlet.app
Once a day the update check reads appcast.xml, with Yardlet's and its updater's version numbers and no system profile. Turn it off in Settings › General. Updates download from GitHub Releases.
Yardlet's relay
Only if you pair a phone or another Mac and it is away from your network. Its messages are end-to-end encrypted; the relay cannot read them and stores none.

How Yardlet uses Google data

Connecting Google is optional, and you choose which of Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive to turn on. Yardlet's own MCP server runs on your Mac and calls Google's APIs directly, only when an agent uses one of these tools, and passes the result only to that agent. Yardlet does not sell Google data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models.

Gmail
Search, read, draft, send and label email. No full mail access, so messages are never deleted for good.
Google Calendar
List calendars, read and change events, and check free times.
Google Drive
Read files, and create or change files of its own.

The privacy policy lists each permission it asks for and where the data goes. Until Google finishes reviewing Yardlet, its sign-in screen shows an “unverified app” warning. You can also sign in through a Google OAuth app of your own.

Good to know

Your plans
Usage counts against your Claude or ChatGPT plan, as it does in the CLI. Yardlet has no account, plan or fee of its own, and the agent's own terms apply.
Requirements
macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon or Intel, and at least one agent CLI installed and signed in, such as codex or claude.
Intel Macs
The Intel version is new: its built-in browser plays no H.264 or AAC, and it has been tried on one Intel Mac so far. Tell us how it goes.
Your accounts
Each task runs the agent's own CLI with the sign-in it already has: the app server for Codex, stream JSON for Claude Code and ACP for the others. Yardlet does not share or proxy accounts.
Updates
Notarized by Apple, and updates itself with Sparkle. It installs only updates signed with Yardlet's release key. Each version's changes are in the changelog.
Native app
Written in Swift and SwiftUI. Its built-in browser panel is Chromium. Windows and Linux: not yet.
