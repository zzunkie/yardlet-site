Yardlet Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

Yardlet is a desktop app that runs on your Mac. It has no analytics and does not send your data to its developer. Its only online services are an update feed and, if you use the phone remote, a relay that passes end-to-end encrypted messages it cannot read.

What Yardlet keeps on your Mac

  • Yardlet's own task list, drafts and reading caches are stored under ~/.yardlet.
  • Conversations with an agent stay in that agent's own session store on your Mac, as they would if you used the agent without Yardlet.
  • Sign-ins for connectors, such as Google, GitHub and Slack, are stored in the macOS Keychain.

Yardlet starts the agent CLIs you have installed with their own sign-in, and does not copy those sign-ins from other apps. If you ask it to, Yardlet copies a website's sign-in from another browser on your Mac into its built-in browser; that sign-in stays on your Mac. The built-in browser keeps its cookies and site data on your Mac, and items you save in Yardlet's vault are stored in the macOS Keychain.

Agents and AI providers

Yardlet runs coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex. Each agent works under your own account with its AI provider (for example Anthropic for Claude Code, or OpenAI for Codex). What you type in a task, and what the agent reads while working on it, is sent by that agent to the provider you chose, under your account. That includes data the agent reads through a connector, such as an email, a calendar event or a Drive file.

Whether that provider may use the data to train or improve its models is decided by the settings and terms of your account with that provider, not by Yardlet. Yardlet itself never uses your data to train AI models. Check your provider account's data settings before you let an agent read data you do not want that provider to receive.

Google user data

Yardlet asks for access to Google services only when you connect a Google account in its settings. Depending on the services you turn on, it requests these permissions:

  • Gmail (gmail.modify): read, search, draft, send and label your email. Yardlet does not ask for full mail access, so it cannot permanently delete your email.
  • Google Calendar (calendar.calendarlist.readonly, calendar.events, calendar.events.freebusy): list your calendars, read and change events, and check free/busy times.
  • Google Drive (drive.readonly, drive.file): read your files, and create or change files that Yardlet creates.

How Google data is used

Yardlet calls Google's APIs directly from your Mac, only when a coding agent you are running asks for it through Yardlet's tools. It passes the result only to that agent, so the agent can complete the task you gave it. The result then becomes part of that conversation:

  • The agent sends it to its AI provider under your account, as described in Agents and AI providers. That provider's terms and privacy policy, and your account's training settings with it, apply to what the agent receives.
  • The agent and Yardlet keep the conversation on your Mac. Yardlet does not copy Google data to any server.

Yardlet does not sell Google user data, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train or improve AI models. The developer never receives your Google data, so no person working on Yardlet can read it.

Storage and removal

Your Google sign-in tokens are stored in the macOS Keychain on your Mac. Removing the Google account in Yardlet's settings deletes the stored sign-in. If you delete the Yardlet app without removing the account first, the sign-in stays in your Keychain until you remove it in Keychain Access.

Removing the account in Yardlet does not cancel the access you granted at Google. You can revoke Yardlet's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Other connectors

GitHub, Slack and the other services you connect work the same way. The sign-in is stored in the macOS Keychain, calls go straight from your Mac to that service, and what an agent reads through them is sent to that agent's provider as described above. Each service's own privacy policy applies to your account there.

Updates

Once a day, unless you turn it off in Settings > General, Yardlet reads https://yardlet.app/appcast.xml to see whether a newer version exists. The request carries Yardlet's and its updater's version numbers and no system profile. Updates download from GitHub Releases, which may log the download as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Phone remote

If you pair a phone with Yardlet and the phone is away from your Mac's network, the two connect through Yardlet's relay at relay.yardlet.app, which runs on Cloudflare. Everything between the phone and your Mac, such as tasks, conversations and approvals, is end-to-end encrypted with keys only the two devices hold, so the relay cannot read it and stores none of it. The relay sees the connection itself: a room identifier, IP addresses, when devices connect and how much they send. Notifications for the phone are encrypted the same way before they pass through the relay and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging; to deliver one, the relay uses the phone's notification token and keeps it only for that moment. If you sign in to the relay with GitHub or Google to find your Mac from anywhere, it keeps your Macs' names and public keys under that account.

Limited Use

Yardlet's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

This website

This website has no analytics, cookies, third-party scripts or requests to other sites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may log visits as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.
